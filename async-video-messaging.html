Async video messaging: say it once, skip the meeting

Most meetings are one person talking while everyone else listens. Async video messaging flips that: you record the talking part once, send it, and everyone watches when it suits them. The meeting only happens if a real discussion is needed.

What "async" actually means

Not "slow". It means the sender and the watcher don't have to be in the same place at the same time. You record a two-minute walkthrough at 9am; your teammate in another time zone watches it at 4pm and replies with their own two-minute video. No calendar invites, no "can you hear me", no sitting through a call for the one minute that concerned you.

Messages that work better as video

  • Bug reports: show the bug happening, narrate the steps. A developer fixes it twice as fast as from a written ticket.
  • Product demos: one recording, sent to ten prospects, watched whenever they like.
  • Feedback: point at the design and talk. Tone of voice does what bold text cannot.
  • Onboarding: record the walkthrough once; every new joiner watches the same good version.
  • Status updates: a three-minute video beats a thirty-minute standup that was mostly waiting.

How to record a good async video

  • One idea per video. If it needs a second idea, record a second video.
  • Show, don't describe. Put the thing on screen and point at it.
  • Keep it under five minutes. If it runs longer, it is probably a meeting in disguise.
  • End with the ask. Say what you need back, and by when: "Reply with your thoughts by Thursday."
  • Don't re-record for small stumbles. Pause, breathe, continue. People forgive a pause; they don't forgive a ten-minute ramble.
